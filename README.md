# UnderScope

### See what the market is missing.

UnderScope is an AI-powered financial research platform built to help investors look beyond stock prices and financial statements.

Most financial tools answer questions like **"What happened?"**

UnderScope focuses on a different question:

> **"What might I be missing?"**

The idea is simple: companies don't operate in isolation. A change in a supplier, commodity, currency, regulation, competitor, or macroeconomic condition can create a chain of effects that eventually reaches the business.

UnderScope brings these relationships together so users can investigate those connections instead of viewing financial information in isolation.

---

## What it does

### Company Intelligence

A single research interface for exploring a company across:

* Company fundamentals
* Financial metrics
* Market data
* Events
* Investigations
* Dependencies
* Scenario analysis

### Investigations

UnderScope identifies areas that may deserve further investigation, including:

* Hidden risks
* Business dependencies
* Contradictions
* Competitive pressure
* Regulatory exposure
* Second-order effects

Investigations include supporting evidence, confidence levels, relevant metrics, and an explanation of why the finding could matter.

### Dependency Network

Companies are connected to much more than their immediate competitors.

UnderScope maps relationships involving:

* Suppliers
* Customers
* Competitors
* Commodities
* Countries
* Currencies
* Regulation
* Infrastructure

Users can explore these relationships and understand why each dependency matters.

### Ripple Effects

One of the core ideas behind UnderScope is that an external event rarely affects a company in isolation.

For example:

```text
Oil prices increase
        ↓
Input costs increase
        ↓
Margins come under pressure
        ↓
Pricing changes
        ↓
Consumer demand changes
```

UnderScope helps users investigate these chains and understand how a change in one part of the market could propagate through a business.

---

## Why I built it

Financial research is often fragmented across multiple platforms.

You might have financial statements in one place, market data somewhere else, news somewhere else, and industry research somewhere else.

But some of the most important things to understand about a company are the relationships between those pieces of information.

For example, a company may appear financially strong while being heavily dependent on a particular supplier, geographic region, commodity, or customer group.

I wanted to build a tool that makes those relationships easier to explore.

That became the idea behind UnderScope.

---

## How it is different

UnderScope isn't intended to be another stock dashboard or a tool that tells users what stocks to buy.

Instead, it is designed around **investigation**.

The goal is to help users move from:

**"What happened?"**

to:

**"What is this company exposed to?"**

and ultimately:

**"What could happen next, and what should I investigate?"**
